Do language models organize mathematics geometrically?

Libor “Burny” BurianOctober 2026 Paper (PDF) Code & preregistration Data & activations

LLMs put the days of the week on a circle (Engels et al., 2024). We asked whether abstract mathematics has a shape too: are axioms directions, is there a map of mathematical fields, and are chains of definitions laid out in order? Each of five open base models is compared with its random-init twin, the same network and tokenizer with untrained weights.

TL;DR

  • Axioms act as directions. “Add commutativity” is a consistent arrow (offset consistency , twin ), as is “add associativity”; identity and divisibility are weaker.
  • Fields form a map. Distances between field centroids correlate with arXiv cross-listing: ρ = in the confirmatory model, against for its twin, and in every model we tested.
  • Days go round in order in every model, months in most (the positive control, post-hoc); class-inclusion chains are ordered in the confirmatory model and pass the full preregistered test in most models (scorecard). Angles are not circles, which falsified one of our preregistered predictions.
  • Not special to LLMs or to math. A text-embedding model reaches a similar map (ρ = ), science topics cluster by field even more strongly, and over a trillion tokens of math pretraining did not sharpen it.

1How we look inside the model

For every concept we run eight short sentences that end with its name, for example “In mathematics, one important concept is ⟨concept⟩”. We read the model’s internal vector (the residual stream) at the concept’s last token, and average over the eight templates. The field-map templates never name a field, so a field cannot be read off the prompt. Family items use one fixed domain per family, e.g. “In abstract algebra, consider monoid”, so the domain is the same for every item compared.

Random-init twin. The same architecture and tokenizer with weights redrawn at random. Any geometry it shows comes from word form alone (shared tokens, length). Every claim here is a difference from the twin.

Then we ask simple geometric questions of those vectors. Are differences such as monoid − semigroup parallel to loop − quasigroup? Are related fields close? Do chains of definitions line up in order? All views below are unsupervised projections (PCA, MDS): nothing is fitted to the labels, because with a dozen points in thousands of dimensions a fitted projection can draw any shape.

3Axioms as directions

Thirteen group-like structures, from magma to abelian group, are each a set of four axioms: associativity, identity, divisibility (inverses) and commutativity. If “adding an axiom” is a direction in the model, then every pair of structures that differs only in that axiom should have the same difference vector. Examples are semigroup → monoid, quasigroup → loop and magma → unital magma. This is the parallelogram picture of word analogies (man : woman :: king : queen; Mikolov et al., 2013). walks through the test in four steps; the figures after it show the evidence.

Offset consistency. The mean cosine between same-axiom difference vectors. 0 = unrelated arrows; 1 = parallel. Compared with random pairs of structures, and with the twin.

4A map of mathematics

We drew on average about two dozen concepts (at most 30) from Wikipedia glossaries for each of fields, skipping names that give their field away (“topological space”). We then averaged each field’s concepts into a field centroid. The ground truth for “related fields” is how often mathematicians cross-list papers between the corresponding arXiv categories, over papers.

ρ (RSA). Spearman correlation between all pairwise distances of the model’s field centroids and all pairwise arXiv distances. 1 = the same ranking of field pairs; 0 = unrelated.

6Shapes: circles, lines and a broken circle

Ordered families should trace a path through activation space. Days and months are our positive control: Engels et al. found them on circles. The ring chain (rng ⊃ ring ⊃ commutative ring ⊃ integral domain ⊃ … ⊃ field ⊃ algebraically closed field) and the space chain (T0 ⊃ T1 ⊃ Hausdorff ⊃ … ⊃ Hilbert space) are inclusions of definitions. We predicted that angles from 0° to 330° would close into a circle, with 330° next to 0°.

Cycle score. The fraction of items whose angular neighbour in the PC plane is the true next item. 1 = perfect cyclic order, which an open arc can also reach; the closed-cycle test also requires the loop to close. Post-hoc. Robinson score: the fraction of item triples consistent with the true order; chance is 1/3.

8What did not work

  • Identity and divisibility are weaker directions: they do not survive multiple-comparison correction in every model.
  • No “make the operation total” direction in the partial-operation lattice ().
  • Angles are not a circle in any model, not in the top-2 plane and, under a pre-specified post-hoc test, not significantly in any plane of the top five principal components either. Their geometry follows the bare numerals 0…330 (ρ = in Qwen3.5-2B). This falsifies our preregistered prediction.
  • No clean hierarchy. Under the preregistered cophenetic test, the map does not beat the twin as a five-branch tree.
  • Math pretraining did not sharpen the map. Qwen2.5-Math vs its base: Δρ 95% CI [, ].
  • Not specific to LLMs or to math. Qwen3-Embedding reaches a similar map (Qwen3.5-2B), and science topics cluster by field even more strongly.
  • The ring chain fails the full preregistered test in two models (its leave-one-out order is not significant there; see the scorecard); in Gemma the space chain also misses Holm significance.
  • Causal test of chain geometry inconclusive.
