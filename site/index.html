Do language models organize mathematics geometrically?

Libor “Burny” BurianOctober 2026 Paper (PDF) Code & preregistration Data & activations

LLMs put the days of the week on a circle (Engels et al., 2024). We asked whether abstract mathematics has a shape too: is there a map of mathematical fields, are chains of definitions laid out in order, and are axioms directions? Each of five open base models is compared with its random-init twin, the same network and tokenizer with untrained weights.

TL;DR

  • Fields form a map. Distances between field centroids correlate with arXiv cross-listing: ρ = in the confirmatory model, against for its twin, and in every model we tested.
  • Days and months are circles; class-inclusion chains are lines. Angles are not circles, which falsified one of our preregistered predictions.
  • Axioms act as directions. “Add commutativity” is a consistent arrow (offset consistency ), as is “add associativity”; identity and divisibility are weaker.
  • Not special to LLMs or to math. A text-embedding model reaches a similar map (ρ = ), science topics cluster as strongly, and a trillion tokens of math pretraining did not sharpen it.

1How we look inside the model

For every concept we run eight short sentences that end with its name, for example “In mathematics, one important notion is the ⟨concept⟩”. We read the model’s internal vector (the residual stream) at the concept’s last token, and average over the eight templates. Templates never name a field, so a field cannot be read off the prompt.

Random-init twin. The same architecture and tokenizer with weights redrawn at random. Any geometry it shows comes from word form alone (shared tokens, length). Every claim here is a difference from the twin.

Then we ask simple geometric questions of those vectors. Are related fields close? Do chains of definitions line up in order? Are differences such as monoid − semigroup parallel to loop − quasigroup? All views below are unsupervised projections (PCA, MDS): nothing is fitted to the labels, because with a dozen points in thousands of dimensions a fitted projection can draw any shape.

3A map of mathematics

We drew about two dozen concepts from Wikipedia glossaries for each of fields, skipping names that give their field away (“topological space”). We then averaged each field’s concepts into a field centroid. The ground truth for “related fields” is how often mathematicians cross-list papers between the corresponding arXiv categories, over papers.

ρ (RSA). Spearman correlation between all pairwise distances of the model’s field centroids and all pairwise arXiv distances. 1 = the same ranking of field pairs; 0 = unrelated.

5Shapes: circles, lines and a broken circle

Ordered families should trace a path through activation space. Days and months are our positive control: Engels et al. found them on circles. The ring chain (rng ⊃ ring ⊃ commutative ring ⊃ integral domain ⊃ … ⊃ field) and the space chain (T0 ⊃ T1 ⊃ Hausdorff ⊃ … ⊃ Hilbert space) are inclusions of definitions. We predicted that angles from 0° to 330° would close into a circle, with 330° next to 0°.

Cycle score. The fraction of items whose angular neighbour in the PC plane is the true next item. 1 = perfect cycle. Post-hoc. Robinson score: the fraction of item triples consistent with the true order; chance is 1/3.

6Axioms as directions

Thirteen group-like structures, from magma to abelian group, are each a set of four axioms: associativity, identity, divisibility (inverses) and commutativity. If “adding an axiom” is a direction in the model, then every pair of structures that differs only in that axiom should have the same difference vector. Examples are semigroup → monoid, quasigroup → loop and magma → unital magma. This is the parallelogram picture of word analogies (man : woman :: king : queen).

Offset consistency. The mean cosine between same-axiom difference vectors. 0 = unrelated arrows; 1 = parallel. Compared with random pairs of structures, and with the twin.

7Concrete to abstract

Three LLM judges from model families we do not probe rated every concept’s abstraction from 1 to 7. After removing word frequency and length, the first principal component of the concept cloud correlates with these ratings: ρ = . The second correlates with the pure/applied label. As an outside check, the judges’ ratings rise with how deep a concept’s definition sits in Mathlib’s import graph, though only of concepts could be matched.

8What did not work

  • Angles are not a circle in any model. Their geometry follows the bare numerals 0…330 (ρ = in Qwen3.5-2B). This falsifies our preregistered prediction.
  • No clean hierarchy. Under the preregistered cophenetic test, the map does not beat the twin as a five-branch tree.
  • Math pretraining did not sharpen the map. Qwen2.5-Math vs its base: Δρ 95% CI [, ].
  • Not specific to LLMs or to math. Qwen3-Embedding reaches a similar map, and science topics cluster as strongly.
  • Causal test inconclusive, and identity and divisibility directions do not survive multiple-comparison correction in every model.
